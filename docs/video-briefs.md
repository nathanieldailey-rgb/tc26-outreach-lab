# Explainer Video Briefs

These concepts are production briefs, not finished or approved committee videos. Each is designed for a 60–90 second public explainer, uses original narration, points to DOI records for depth, and can be represented by the site's interactive storyboard before production funding is approved.

## Concept 1: Outreach Is Part of the Safety Architecture

**Duration: 75 seconds**

**Audience:** students, public communicators, policy staff, and new operators

**Learning promise:** explain why shared understanding and feedback affect the quality of space-traffic decisions.

### Storyboard and script

| Time | Visual | Narration | On-screen text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–12s | A sparse orbital line gains vehicles, operators, regulators, and observers. | “Space traffic management is not only a technical calculation. It is a coordination problem shared by people with different roles and different information.” | One environment. Many decisions. |
| 12–28s | One warning passes through several plain-language translations. | “A warning works only when its meaning survives the handoffs—from sensors to operators, and from specialists to decision makers and the public.” | Evidence → meaning → action |
| 28–47s | Two-way arrows connect a public explainer, a classroom, and an operations desk. | “Outreach is therefore more than broadcasting. Questions and misunderstandings reveal where terms, assumptions, and procedures need clarification.” | Outreach is a feedback loop. |
| 47–64s | A source card opens to a DOI pathway while the original explainer stays visible. | “Good outreach shows its sources and sends readers to the committee's full technical work instead of replacing it.” | Read the full paper |
| 64–75s | The network settles into a shared traffic line. | “A safer traffic system begins with a community that can understand, question, and improve it.” | Ask. Read. Contribute. |

### Rights, accessibility, and playback

Use project-original words and commissioned vector shapes only; use no publisher figures, article text, logos, or marks. Record permission for music, voice, icons, and fonts. Burned-in text must also appear in accurate captions, and a text transcript must accompany the video. Describe meaningful motion in the transcript. The embedded player is non-autoplay and begins only after a user action; a static sequence must remain understandable with reduced motion.

## Concept 2: Reentry Does Not End at an Orbital Boundary

**Duration: 80 seconds**

**Audience:** public-safety officials, regulators, journalists, and interested residents

**Learning promise:** show why reentry connects orbital knowledge with terrestrial coordination.

### Storyboard and script

| Time | Visual | Narration | On-screen text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–13s | A tracked object crosses a simple altitude line toward Earth. | “When an object begins reentry, the problem does not disappear with its orbit.” | Reentry connects domains. |
| 13–30s | Uncertainty bands surround a projected path and narrow as observations arrive. | “Observers update a changing estimate: when the object will return, where uncertainty extends, and which pieces may survive.” | Observation changes the estimate. |
| 30–49s | The path reaches aviation, maritime, emergency, and public-information icons. | “Those estimates must reach organizations that use different procedures, timelines, and language.” | Timely coordination matters. |
| 49–67s | A careful message separates known facts, uncertainty, and next update. | “Useful communication distinguishes what is known, what remains uncertain, and when the evidence will be refreshed.” | Known / uncertain / next |
| 67–80s | A DOI source pathway appears beside an invitation to explore. | “The committee's reentry report provides the technical starting point. This explainer helps readers find it and ask better questions.” | Follow the evidence. |

### Rights, accessibility, and playback

Use original generic maps and trajectories; do not imply a forecast for a real event. Use no agency seals, publisher diagrams, full paper text, logos, or marks without documented permission. Provide synchronized captions, an accessible transcript, high-contrast labels, and audio description for visual-only uncertainty changes. The player is non-autoplay, includes pause and scrub controls, and preserves a meaningful static frame under reduced motion.

## Concept 3: Traffic Management Beyond Earth Orbit

**Duration: 75 seconds**

**Audience:** program leaders, emerging-space practitioners, educators, and the public

**Learning promise:** introduce the coordination questions that grow as missions move from Earth orbit toward the Moon and Mars.

### Storyboard and script

| Time | Visual | Narration | On-screen text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–12s | The orbital traffic line extends from Earth toward the Moon. | “The habits built for Earth orbit will influence how missions coordinate farther from home.” | Beyond Earth orbit |
| 12–29s | Different reference frames and communication delays appear as simple clocks and axes. | “Distance changes the picture: reference frames differ, communications take longer, and local conditions shape what can be observed.” | Time, frames, knowledge |
| 29–47s | Scientific, commercial, and public missions approach shared regions without colliding. | “More actors and mission types make shared terminology, data expectations, and responsibility especially important.” | Coordination before congestion |
| 47–63s | Five topic clusters connect along one line rather than forming separate silos. | “The same publication program links foundations, orbital knowledge, operations, governance, and future domains.” | One connected research agenda |
| 63–75s | The 20-record library and Moon-to-Mars DOI pathway come forward. | “Explore the public metadata, read the full papers, and help shape the next question the committee should explain.” | Read. Ask. Shape what comes next. |

### Rights, accessibility, and playback

Create all celestial and trajectory imagery from original geometric assets or document a reusable public-domain source version. Use no mission photography, publisher figures, logos, or marks without written permission. Supply accurate captions, a complete transcript, text alternatives for each storyboard frame, and a narration-free visual description. The experience is non-autoplay with keyboard-operable controls and no timed progression when reduced motion is requested.
